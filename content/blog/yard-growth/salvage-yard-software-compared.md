## Why Does Salvage Yard Software Matter in 2026? {#why-software-matters}

The US vehicle recycling market hit $85.5 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $165 billion by 2033, according to [Verified Market Research](https://www.verifiedmarketresearch.com/product/automobile-recycling-market/) (2024). That kind of growth attracts competition. Software is the baseline that separates yards that scale from yards that stall.

Good salvage yard software handles five core jobs: vehicle acquisition, parts cataloging, interchange lookup, pricing, and sales tracking. Some tools add marketplace syndication, accounting, or fleet management on top.

But here's what matters most. Interchange data. The Hollander interchange system maps which parts fit across makes, models, and years. When a customer asks for a 2017 Ford F-150 headlight, the interchange tells you whether the 2018 or 2019 version is the same part. Without it, you're guessing, and guessing costs sales.

We've talked to hundreds of yard owners through our network, and the pattern is consistent: yards that adopt structured inventory software within their first two years of operation report fewer lost sales and faster part lookups than yards that wait. The ones who wait often describe the transition as "painful but worth it."

Every tool below addresses these core jobs differently. The right pick depends on your yard's size, your primary sales channels, and how much you're willing to spend. Let's walk through each one.


## Hollander/Pinnacle {#hollander-pinnacle}

Hollander has been the backbone of salvage yard data since the 1930s. Their interchange numbering system is the industry standard. Pinnacle is Hollander's full yard management platform, and it's the most established name in this space.

### How it works

Pinnacle runs the entire dismantling workflow, from vehicle check-in through parts sales. You scan a VIN, and the system pulls interchange data, identifies saleable parts, and generates pull lists. It integrates directly with Hollander's proprietary interchange database, the same database that most other tools license or reference.

Pinnacle also handles yard mapping, warranty tracking, and core management. For yards that need to report to state agencies or manage environmental compliance, the reporting tools cover that too.

### Best for

Large, established yards that want a proven, full-featured platform. Multi-location operations benefit from Pinnacle's centralized data architecture. If you've been in business for more than a decade, there's a good chance you've already encountered Hollander's interchange numbers.

### Pricing

Quote-based. Pricing depends on yard size, user count, and modules. Expect enterprise-level pricing. Hollander doesn't publish rates, and implementation costs can be significant because the system is comprehensive.

**Citation capsule**: Hollander's interchange numbering system, introduced in the 1930s, remains the de facto standard for cross-referencing auto parts across makes, models, and years. Pinnacle, Hollander's yard management platform, integrates this interchange data directly into the dismantling workflow, making it the most data-rich option for large salvage operations ([Hollander, 2025](https://www.hollanderparts.com/)).

## Dismantly {#dismantly}

Dismantly is one of the newer names in salvage yard software. It launched with a cloud-first approach and leans heavily on AI-assisted part identification. If your yard sells through eBay, Facebook Marketplace, or other online channels, this one's worth a close look.

### How it works

You photograph a vehicle or part, and Dismantly's system suggests what to pull based on current market demand. It generates listings and syndicates them across multiple marketplaces from one dashboard. Update a price or mark a part as sold, and the change pushes everywhere.

Inspection checklists help standardize how your team grades parts, which matters when you've got three different people pulling inventory and each one has a different idea of what "good condition" means.

### Best for

Small to mid-size yards that sell heavily online. If you're listing 50 or more parts per week on eBay and Facebook, Dismantly's multi-channel sync saves real hours.

### Pricing

Monthly subscription, tiered by inventory volume and marketplace integrations. Generally mid-range. Expect $100 to $300 per month depending on your plan.

I've seen yards cut their listing time in half after switching to a tool like Dismantly. The AI cataloging isn't perfect, it sometimes misidentifies trim levels, but it's faster than manual data entry for 90% of common parts.

**Citation capsule**: Dismantly uses AI-assisted part identification and multi-channel marketplace syndication to help salvage yards list parts across eBay, Facebook Marketplace, and other platforms from a single dashboard. Its cloud-based architecture provides mobile access, making it particularly suited for yards with fewer than 500 vehicles that prioritize online sales ([Dismantly, 2026](https://dismantly.com/)).

## YardSmart {#yardsmart}

YardSmart targets the multi-yard operator. If you're running two, three, or five locations, the challenge isn't just tracking parts. It's knowing which yard has the part a customer needs right now.

### How it works

YardSmart integrates Hollander interchange data and tracks inventory across multiple locations with a centralized view. It handles yard-to-yard transfers, so when Location A gets a request for a part that's sitting at Location B, the system shows it immediately.

Reporting is YardSmart's other strength. You can break down which vehicles and parts generate the most revenue, by location, by month, by category. That data feeds smarter buying decisions at auction.

### Best for

Multi-location operations. If you're running a single yard with under 300 vehicles, YardSmart is more tool than you need. But if you've got two or more lots, the centralized inventory view pays for itself quickly.

### Pricing

Quote-based. Pricing scales with yard count, user seats, and modules. Expect it to climb fast once you start adding locations and features.

**Citation capsule**: YardSmart provides multi-yard inventory management with Hollander interchange integration, enabling centralized part tracking and yard-to-yard transfers. It is primarily designed for salvage operations running two or more locations that need consolidated reporting across all sites ([YardSmart, 2026](https://yardsmart.com/)).

## Car-Part Checkmate {#checkmate}

Car-Part.com is the largest used auto parts search engine in the US, indexing over 200 million parts from thousands of yards ([Car-Part.com](https://www.car-part.com/), 2025). Checkmate is their inventory management tool, and its biggest advantage is obvious: it feeds your parts directly into that massive search network.

### How it works

Checkmate manages your yard's inventory and pushes it straight to Car-Part.com's buyer network. When a shop or DIY customer searches Car-Part.com for a 2016 Honda Civic alternator, your inventory shows up if you've got one. No separate listing step. Interchange data is built in, sourced from Car-Part.com's own extensive database.

The system also handles core tracking, customer management, and basic reporting. It's not trying to be a full business management suite. It's trying to connect your parts with the largest pool of buyers searching for them.

### Best for

Yards where the majority of remote sales come through Car-Part.com searches. If you already get 40% or more of your phone calls from Car-Part.com listings, Checkmate tightens that pipeline significantly.

### Pricing

Subscription-based, often bundled with Car-Part.com listing fees. Mid-range, similar to Dismantly. The bundled model can be cost-effective if Car-Part.com is your primary channel.

**Citation capsule**: Car-Part Checkmate provides native integration with Car-Part.com's 200-million-part search network. Yards using Checkmate push inventory updates directly to where millions of monthly buyer searches happen, eliminating the delay between cataloging a part and making it visible to buyers ([Car-Part.com, 2025](https://www.car-part.com/)).


## Frazer DMS {#frazer}

Frazer has been building dealer management software for decades. Their DMS covers the full business cycle, from buying vehicles at auction to selling parts to closing the books at year end. It's the old guard, and it's still standing for a reason.

### How it works

Frazer runs on desktop, not the cloud. You install it locally, and it handles vehicle acquisition, dismantling workflows, parts inventory, sales, and, this is the big differentiator, integrated accounting. If you're running QuickBooks alongside your inventory system and manually reconciling between them, Frazer eliminates that step.

Title management is built in, too. For yards that buy vehicles at auction, having acquisition paperwork, title processing, and inventory all in one system reduces the paperwork shuffle.

### Best for

Yards that want one system for everything, especially those that also buy and sell whole vehicles alongside parts. If your accountant is tired of importing data from two different programs, Frazer's integrated books are the selling point.

### Pricing

Quote-based with licensing fees. Desktop installation means no monthly cloud subscription, but you'll pay for updates and support. Implementation costs vary.

**Citation capsule**: Frazer DMS is a desktop-based dealer management system covering the complete salvage yard business cycle, including vehicle acquisition, parts inventory, sales, and integrated accounting. Its decades-long track record makes it the most established all-in-one option for yards that want financial reporting and inventory management in a single installed system ([Frazer Computing, 2026](https://www.frazer.com/)).

## CRUSH (IAA) {#crush}

CRUSH is IAA's (Insurance Auto Auctions) yard management platform. If you're already buying vehicles through IAA auctions, CRUSH ties your acquisition pipeline directly into your dismantling and inventory workflow.

### How it works

CRUSH connects to IAA's auction platform, so vehicles you buy flow automatically into your yard management system. From there, it handles dismantling schedules, parts inventory, and sales tracking. The integration with IAA's auction data means you can track acquisition costs per vehicle and measure actual ROI on each car you dismantle.

The system also supports environmental compliance reporting, which matters in states with strict regulations around fluid disposal and hazardous materials.

### Best for

Yards that source heavily from IAA auctions. If 50% or more of your vehicles come through IAA, the seamless auction-to-yard pipeline eliminates manual data entry from that process. Also useful for yards in states with strict environmental compliance requirements.

### Pricing

Quote-based. Pricing typically connects to your IAA buyer relationship. Contact IAA directly for current rates.

CRUSH is often overlooked in software comparisons because it's tied to an auction house rather than positioned as standalone yard software. But for yards that already buy through IAA, the acquisition-to-inventory automation is genuinely valuable, possibly the strongest single integration on this list for that specific workflow.

**Citation capsule**: CRUSH, operated by IAA (Insurance Auto Auctions), integrates the vehicle acquisition pipeline directly with yard management. Vehicles purchased at IAA auctions flow into inventory automatically, enabling yards to track per-vehicle acquisition costs and dismantling ROI without manual data re-entry ([IAA, 2026](https://www.iaai.com/)).

## Yard Manager {#yard-manager}

Yard Manager is the budget option, and I don't mean that as an insult. Not every yard needs enterprise software. If you're moving from spreadsheets to your first real system, Yard Manager covers the basics without the sticker shock.

### How it works

Parts tracking, pull lists, dismantle lists, and basic inventory search. That's the core. You log vehicles, track which parts have been pulled, schedule what's next for dismantling, and look up what's in stock when a customer calls.

It doesn't do marketplace syndication. It doesn't have deep interchange data. It doesn't handle accounting. What it does, it does simply, and for a lot of small yards, that's enough.

### Best for

Smaller yards with under 200 vehicles that need structured inventory tracking at a low cost. A solid stepping stone for yards that plan to upgrade to a more feature-rich platform later.

### Pricing

The most budget-friendly option in this comparison. Subscription or license model, typically under $100 per month. Some plans run even lower depending on feature tier.


**Citation capsule**: Yard Manager provides foundational inventory management, including parts tracking, pull lists, and dismantle scheduling, at the lowest price point among major salvage yard software tools. It is best suited for single-location yards with fewer than 200 vehicles transitioning from spreadsheets to structured software ([SalvageSoftware, 2026](https://salvagesoftware.com/)).

## How Do You Choose the Right Junkyard Management Tool? {#how-to-choose}

According to the [IBISWorld Auto Parts Recycling industry report](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-research-reports/auto-parts-recycling-industry/) (2025), the average US salvage yard generates around $1.2 million in annual revenue. Your software should match the scale and complexity of that revenue, not exceed it.

Three factors should drive your decision.

**Your primary sales channel.** If you sell 60% of your parts through online marketplaces, Dismantly's multi-channel sync is worth the subscription. If Car-Part.com drives most of your remote inquiries, Checkmate's native integration is hard to beat. If you sell mostly to walk-ins and phone callers, you don't need marketplace features at all.

**Your yard count.** Single yard under 300 vehicles? Start with Yard Manager or Dismantly and upgrade when you outgrow them. Multiple locations? YardSmart or Hollander/Pinnacle from day one. The cost of running disconnected systems across multiple lots adds up faster than you'd expect.

**Your budget.** Yard Manager gets you in the door for under $100 per month. Dismantly and Checkmate sit in the mid-range. YardSmart, Frazer, Hollander/Pinnacle, and CRUSH cost more but cover more ground. Match the investment to your revenue, not your ambition. You can always upgrade.

What about interchange data? If the tool you're evaluating doesn't include Hollander or equivalent interchange lookup, think twice. Interchange is how you turn one part number into five compatible listings across model years. Without it, you're [leaving sales on the table](/blog/yard-growth/sell-more-parts).


## Software Manages Inventory, a Hotline Finds Buyers {#software-plus-hotline}

Every tool in this comparison solves the same fundamental problem: knowing what's on your lot. That's table stakes. You can't run a serious yard without it. But inventory software doesn't generate demand. It organizes your supply.

Here's the gap. A buyer has to find your listing, click on it, and call you. That works for high-demand parts. But what about the 2014 Chevy Cruze rear bumper that's been sitting in your warehouse for three months? No one's searching for it. Your listing data goes stale between updates. The part collects dust.

A [voice-based parts network](/blog/yard-growth/how-to-network-with-other-salvage-yards) works the other side. A yard broadcasts a parts request to every connected yard in the region. If you've got it, you respond in seconds. No listing required. No waiting for a search algorithm to surface your part. The average broadcast-to-response time on live voice networks is under 5 seconds, fast enough to close a deal before the caller moves on.

We've watched yards run Checkmate or Dismantly for inventory and use a voice network to move the parts that listings alone never sold. The software tracks what you have. The hotline puts a buyer's voice in your ear asking for exactly that part. They're not competing tools. They're complementary.

The yards moving the most parts in 2026 are stacking channels: walk-in traffic, online marketplaces, Car-Part.com listings, and live voice networks. No single tool covers all of that. The smart play is to pick the inventory software that fits your yard, then add demand-generation tools on top. ## FAQ {#faq}

**What is the best salvage yard software for a small yard?**

For a yard with under 500 vehicles, Yard Manager or Dismantly offer the lowest barrier to entry. Yard Manager covers the basics, pull lists and dismantle scheduling, at the lowest monthly cost. Dismantly adds AI-assisted cataloging and marketplace sync if you sell online. Both beat spreadsheets on day one.

**Does salvage yard software replace a parts hotline?**

No. They solve different problems. Software tracks what's on your lot. A voice hotline broadcasts live buyer requests to hundreds of yards simultaneously. Most yards run both. Software organizes your supply, and the hotline surfaces demand you'd never see from a listing alone. For more on how networking works, read our [guide to yard-to-yard networking](/blog/yard-growth/how-to-network-with-other-salvage-yards).


**How much does auto dismantler inventory software cost?**

Pricing ranges widely. Yard Manager starts under $100 per month. Cloud tools like Dismantly and Checkmate run $100 to $300 per month depending on features. YardSmart, Frazer, Hollander/Pinnacle, and CRUSH use quote-based pricing that scales with yard count and modules. Most vendors require a demo before quoting.

**What is the Hollander interchange system, and why does it matter?**

The Hollander interchange system maps which parts are compatible across different makes, models, and years. It's been the industry standard since the 1930s. When a customer asks for a part from a 2017 F-150, interchange data tells you whether the 2016 or 2018 version fits. Without it, you lose cross-reference sales. Most serious salvage yard software includes Hollander data or an equivalent.

**Can I switch salvage yard software without losing my inventory data?**

Yes, but expect a migration effort. Most vendors offer data import tools or migration assistance. The pain depends on how clean your current data is. Yards moving from spreadsheets to structured software often spend a week or two on data cleanup. Yards switching between established platforms can usually export and import with vendor support. Ask about migration before you commit to any tool. The cost of switching later is real, which is why [buying the right vehicles upfront](/blog/yard-growth/how-to-buy-salvage-cars-for-dismantling) and tracking them properly from day one matters.
